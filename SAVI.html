SOIL ADJUSTED VEGETATION INDEX (SAVI)
Short Description
The Soil-Adjusted Vegetation Index (SAVI) is a remote sensing tool used to estimate vegetation "greenness" while minimizing the misleading effects of soil brightness. It is an enchancement of the widely used NDVI and is particularly valuavle in arid or semi-arid regions where sparse plant cover leaves much of the soil exposed
Why SAVI instead of NDVI?
SAVI (Soil Adjusted Vegetation Index) is used instead of NDVI primarily to minimize background soil brightness in areas with sparse vegetation (LESS than-40% cover), providing more accurate results in arid or semi-arid regions. While NDVI saturates in high-density vegetation, SAVI offers better sensitivity, making it superior for distinguishing vegetation health.

Tools and Data
  • Tools : Google Earth Engine, ArcGIS PRO
  • Method : SAVI = (NIR-red)/(NIR+red+L)x(1+L)
  • Data Source : Landsat 9, Primary data extracted from Landsat 9 Imagery, USGS, processed using Google Earth Engine
Functions
  1. ESoil Background Correction: NDVI is highly sensitive to the soil color and moisture underneath the vegetation. SAVI introduces a soil adjustment factor (L) to correct this, reducing soil noise.
  2. Ideal for Low Vegetation Areas: In arid regions or early-season agricultural monitoring, the soil is often visible. SAVI is much better at identifying sparse vegetation from the bright, reflective soil background.
